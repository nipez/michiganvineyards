Old Mission vs. Leelanau: Which Peninsula Should You Visit?

January 8, 2026 7 min read By the MV Editorial Team
Home›The Vine›Old Mission vs. Leelanau

Two peninsulas reach north from Traverse City into the cold, clear waters of Grand Traverse Bay, and together they form the beating heart of Michigan wine country. Old Mission and Leelanau share the same 45th-parallel latitude, the same lake-moderated climate, and the same knack for aromatic, cool-climate wines. Yet visit both and you will feel the difference within an hour.

So which one deserves your weekend? We have driven every mile of both wine trails (a genuinely difficult assignment) to break down how they compare on wines, wineries, scenery, and logistics — and to help you choose the peninsula that fits the trip you actually want.

The Peninsulas at a Glance

If you only remember one thing: Old Mission is compact, intimate, and Riesling-obsessed; Leelanau is bigger, more varied, and the place to chase sparkling wine and Pinot Noir. The table below sums up the headline differences.

  Old Mission Peninsula Leelanau Peninsula
SizeOne 19-mile stripBroad, ~30-mile peninsula
Wineries~10–11~25–30
Best known forRiesling, ice wine, bay viewsSparkling, Pinot Noir, variety
VibeIntimate, easy to drive end-to-endSprawling, loop-and-explore
Ideal forA focused half-day or dayA full day or a whole weekend

Old Mission Peninsula: Intimate, Iconic, Riesling-Forward

Old Mission is the peninsula that put Michigan on the wine map. In 1974, Edward O'Keefe planted vinifera on its glacial hills against every bit of conventional wisdom, and the Rieslings that followed rewrote the rules. Today the Old Mission Peninsula AVA packs roughly a dozen wineries into a single 19-mile drive, which makes it the easiest wine trail in the state to tackle end-to-end.

Because the peninsula is so slender, the bay is almost always in view. Riesling is the signature grape — crisp, mineral, and age-worthy — but you will also find excellent Pinot Grigio, Chardonnay, and some of Michigan's best ice wine.

Other must-visits include Chateau Chantal, perched at the peninsula's highest point with 360-degree water views and an on-site inn, and 2 Lads Winery, a modern, architecturally striking estate known for sparkling wine and Cabernet Franc.

Leelanau Peninsula: Bigger, More Varied, Sparkling-Savvy

Cross back through Traverse City and head up the other arm of the bay and you reach the Leelanau Peninsula AVA — Michigan's largest and fastest-growing wine region. With around 25 to 30 wineries spread across a much broader landscape of lakes, orchards, and rolling hills, Leelanau rewards a slower, loop-and-explore approach.

The variety here is the draw. You will find serious sparkling wine, elegant Pinot Noir, aromatic whites, and even orange and natural wines. It is also home to charming villages like Suttons Bay and Leland, making it easy to build a full day around tastings, lunch, and a lakeshore stop.

For bubbles, make a beeline for L. Mawby, Michigan's dedicated sparkling-wine house, and don't miss pioneer estate Leelanau Cellars for its lakeside tasting room.

"Old Mission is the peninsula you can master in an afternoon. Leelanau is the one you keep coming back to."

— MV Editorial Team

How to Choose the Right Peninsula for You

Pick Old Mission if you have limited time, you love Riesling, you want water views at nearly every stop, and you would rather go deep on a handful of estates than race between dozens.

Pick Leelanau if you want the widest range of wine styles, you are chasing sparkling or Pinot Noir, you like pairing tastings with villages and beaches, or you simply have a full day (or a whole weekend) to explore.

Traveling with a mixed group? Leelanau's variety tends to keep everyone happy. Planning a focused, low-stress tasting day? Old Mission is hard to beat. For the full picture of both trails, see our Michigan wine trails guide and trip planner.

The Verdict

There is no wrong answer here — this is one of North America's most underrated wine destinations, and both peninsulas are worth your time. If you can only choose one, let it come down to your palate and your pace: Old Mission for an intimate, Riesling-focused day with the bay always in view; Leelanau for a varied, sparkling-and-Pinot weekend of exploration.

Better yet? Base yourself in Traverse City and do both. They are only a short drive apart, and tasting them back-to-back is the best way to understand what makes northern Michigan wine so special. Start by browsing the full winery directory to map your route.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for Riesling, Old Mission or Leelanau?
Both are excellent, but Old Mission has the deeper Riesling reputation, dating back to Chateau Grand Traverse in the 1970s. Leelanau also makes superb Riesling alongside a broader range of styles.

How many wineries are on each peninsula?
Old Mission has around 10 to 11 wineries in a compact 19-mile strip; Leelanau has roughly 25 to 30 across a much larger area.

Can you visit both peninsulas in one trip?
Absolutely. Both start in Traverse City and are only about a 30 to 40 minute drive apart, so many visitors spend one day on each.
