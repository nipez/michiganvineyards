How Michigan Wine Tours Work

Pick a region, then build a short loop

Most successful Michigan wine tours stay inside one peninsula or AVA for the day. Crossing half the state between tastings burns the afternoon.

Michigan wine country stretches from Traverse City’s twin peninsulas to southwest lakeshore towns and the Tip of the Mitt. A “Michigan wine tour” is usually a self-guided string of tasting rooms along a wine trail — not a single statewide bus route.

Start with the region that matches your base: Traverse City for Old Mission or Leelanau, Saugatuck / Fennville / St. Joseph for Lake Michigan Shore, Petoskey for Tip of the Mitt. Then open the matching trail page and the directory map to cluster stops.

Ready-made day and weekend outlines live on Plan Your Visit. This page focuses on touring strategy: how many stops, when to reserve, and which trail guides to open next.

Two ways to tour
  • Self-guided — Drive (or rideshare where available), follow a trail page, and book tasting appointments yourself. Best flexibility across every Michigan region.
  • Guided (where available) — A local operator handles transport and pacing. We only list partners we actually host on this site — see guided options below.
Practical Tips

Before you pour

Small logistics choices decide whether a Michigan wine tour feels relaxed or rushed.

Designated driver first

Three tasting rooms can mean a dozen pours. Assign a sober driver, book a guided tour where one exists, or use rideshare only where coverage is real — rural peninsulas are spotty.

Tour vs. self-drive

Guided tours shine for groups and Traverse City weekends. Self-drive wins when you want specific estates, cideries, or a Fennville / LMS loop on your own timetable.

Reservations

Summer Saturdays and fall color weekends fill tasting rooms. Check each winery page for hours, then confirm on the winery’s own site or by phone before you go.

How many stops

Plan 3–4 tasting rooms for a full day, or 2–3 if you want lunch and a long patio stop. Compact trails like Old Mission make four stops realistic; spread-out regions need fewer.

Best seasons

May–October is peak. Shoulder months are quieter. Many Traverse City and southwest rooms stay open year-round — confirm winter hours on each listing.

Stay on one map

Use directory map view filtered by region so you are not zigzagging. Pair it with the matching wine trail page for context.

Old Mission Peninsula wine tours

Leelanau Peninsula wine tours

Michigan’s densest trail — sparkling specialists, estate rooms, and Sleeping Bear nearby. Pick a north or south cluster rather than trying the whole peninsula in one day.

Lake Michigan Shore wine tours

Warmer lakeshore AVA for Cabernet Franc and longer-season reds — Baroda, Buchanan, Berrien Springs, Paw Paw. Strong Chicago or Grand Rapids weekend base.

Fennville AVA

Fennville & Saugatuck-area tours

Nested AVA north of the broader LMS — sandy soils, whites, sparkling, and cider. Easy day from Holland or Saugatuck; continue south if you want LMS reds.

Petoskey wine region tours

Northern frontier tasting — cold-hardy hybrids, meaderies, and Up North scenery around Petoskey and Harbor Springs. Best as a vacation add-on, not a rushed day trip from Detroit.
