Michigan Wine for the Holidays: Thanksgiving Pairings & Gift Ideas

November 17, 2026 9 min read By the MV Editorial Team
Home›The Vine›Michigan Wine for the Holidays

Holiday tables in Michigan deserve bottles grown here. This Michigan wine holiday guide covers what to pour with Thanksgiving and Christmas dinners, gift ideas you can pick up at tasting rooms, and how to enjoy a festive winery visit — without inventing prices, awards, or event dates that change every year.

If you need the basics of a tasting-room visit first, start with our Michigan wine tasting guide. Planning a colder-month day trip? See Michigan wineries in winter. Choosing a Traverse City peninsula? Compare them in Old Mission vs. Leelanau, or sketch stops with the wineries map guide.

What to Pour at Thanksgiving

Thanksgiving is a parade of sweet, savory, herbal, and rich flavors on one plate. Cool-climate Michigan wines — aromatic whites, brighter reds, sparkling, and hard cider — tend to keep up better than heavy, high-alcohol bottles. A simple approach:

  • Open with bubbles. Sparkling wine resets the palate while people arrive and snack.
  • Put Riesling (or Gewürztraminer) next to the bird. Aromatic whites love turkey, stuffing herbs, and cranberry.
  • Offer a food-friendly red. Pinot Noir or Cabernet Franc for guests who want something darker without weighing down the meal.
  • Save ice wine or late-harvest for dessert. Or pour hard cider with pie if your table leans orchard.

"You do not need a dozen bottles for Thanksgiving — one sparkling, one aromatic white, one cool-climate red, and a dessert pour cover almost every plate on the table."

— MV Editorial Team

Michigan Styles for the Holiday Table

Here is how Michigan’s best-known styles usually land with holiday food — with winery examples taken from our directory listings. Availability changes by vintage; ask what is pouring or in stock when you visit or shop.

Riesling

Riesling is Michigan’s calling card and a Thanksgiving workhorse. Dry to off-dry bottles cut through gravy and rich sides; a touch of sweetness flatters cranberry and glazed vegetables. Look to estates known for Riesling such as Chateau Grand Traverse on Old Mission (Riesling, Late Harvest Riesling, Ice Wine) or Fenn Valley Vineyards in Fennville.

Sparkling Wine

Sparkling wine is the easiest “welcome pour” of the season and a natural New Year’s companion. For a dedicated sparkling house on Leelanau, visit L. Mawby, known for Méthode Champenoise and Blanc de Blancs. Shady Lane Cellars lists Sparkling Riesling, and Fennville’s Fenn Valley is known for Capriccio sparkling.

Pinot Noir

Michigan Pinot Noir is typically lighter and brighter than warmer-climate versions — a good match for turkey, mushroom dishes, and roasted root vegetables. Estates known for Pinot Noir include Bel Lago on Leelanau and 2 Lads Winery on Old Mission (Cabernet Franc and Pinot Noir among its known wines).

Cabernet Franc

Cabernet Franc often shows herbal and red-fruit notes that sit well beside stuffing, green beans, and herb-roasted meats. On Old Mission, Mari Vineyards is known for Cabernet Franc alongside Nebbiolo and Sangiovese; 2 Lads also lists Cabernet Franc among its known wines.

Gewürztraminer

Gewürztraminer’s spice and floral character loves holiday sides with cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger, or a hint of heat. Old Mission’s Peninsula Cellars and Brys Estate both list Gewürztraminer among their known wines; on Leelanau, Bel Lago does as well.

Ice Wine

Ice wine is a classic Michigan dessert pour — concentrated, cold-weather sweetness for pies, cookies, and cheese boards. Directory listings known for ice wine include Chateau Grand Traverse, Black Star Farms — Leelanau (A Capella Ice Wine), and Southwest Michigan’s 12 Corners Vineyards (Vidal Blanc Ice Wine). Ask what vintages are available; ice wine is seasonal by nature.

Hard Cider

Hard cider belongs on Michigan holiday tables as much as grape wine does — especially with pork, roasted squash, or apple pie. In Fennville, Virtue Cider is known for Michigan Brut, Lapinette, and Percheron. On Leelanau, Tandem Ciders is an artisan cidery known for farmhouse cider and related pours.

Michigan Wine Gift Ideas

A tasting-room visit is one of the easiest ways to shop for the holidays. Exact offerings change by winery — we do not invent prices or club tiers here — but these gift types show up widely across Michigan wine country:

  • Bottles with a story. Pick a style the recipient already loves (Riesling, sparkling, ice wine) or a mixed three-pack: one white, one red or sparkling, one dessert pour. Estates like Chateau Grand Traverse, L. Mawby, and Black Star Farms — Leelanau are natural places to start browsing.
  • Wine club memberships. Many Michigan wineries run clubs with seasonal shipments and member tastings. Ask in the tasting room how signup works and what the next release includes — a membership is a gift that lasts past December.
  • Gift cards. When you are not sure which bottle to choose, a winery gift card lets the recipient pick their own flight or case. Confirm that cards can be used online, in the tasting room, or both.
  • Winery shop items. Glassware, corkscrews, insulated totes, local foods, and branded apparel fill many tasting-room shops. They travel well and pair nicely with a bottle or two.

Holiday Events & Open-House Visits

From late November through New Year’s, many Michigan wineries host holiday open houses, member gatherings, and decorated tasting rooms. Schedules shift every year — treat our events page as a starting point, then confirm dates, reservations, and public hours on the winery’s site before you go.

A few practical notes for festive visits:

  • Expect busier weekends. The Saturday before Christmas and New Year’s Eve week can feel closer to peak season indoors. Midweek afternoons are often calmer for shopping and tasting.
  • Confirm hours. Winter and holiday schedules shorten or pause more often than summer ones. Our winter guide covers the broader off-season pattern.
  • Book when the room asks you to. Open houses and seated pairings may need a reservation; walk-in bars still exist, but holiday crowds fill them fast.
  • Keep the route short. Two stops plus gift shopping is plenty. Use Plan Your Visit for lodging and day-of logistics.

Touring Old Mission & Leelanau for the Holidays

Traverse City’s two peninsulas are a natural base for a holiday wine weekend — compact roads, indoor tasting rooms, and plenty of bottle-shop energy. If you are choosing a single peninsula day, read Old Mission vs. Leelanau; for a mapped route, see our map planning article.

Prefer not to drive between pours — especially after dark or in lake-effect weather? A guided Old Mission / Leelanau outing with Traverse City Wine Tours handles hotel pickup and pacing so your group can taste and shop without designating a driver. Self-guided ideas also live on our wine tours page.

Ready for the Holidays?

Stock the table with Michigan styles you already love, leave room for one new bottle from a tasting room, and treat gift shopping as part of the visit — not a separate errand.

  • Browse the winery directory for Riesling, sparkling, ice wine, and cider producers
  • Check events for seasonal programming, then confirm on each winery’s site
  • Sketch a short route with the wineries map guide or Plan Your Visit
  • Book a guided Traverse City day at traversecitywinetour.com when you want the driving handled
  • Brush up on tasting-room basics in our first-timers tasting guide

Michigan wine country shines when the weather cools and the table fills up. Pour something grown here — and bring a bottle home for someone else, too.
