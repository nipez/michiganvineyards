Michigan Wineries Open in Winter: An Off-Season Tasting Guide

November 3, 2026 8 min read By the MV Editorial Team
Home›The Vine›Michigan Wineries in Winter

Looking for Michigan wineries open in winter? You are not alone. From roughly November through March, wine country sheds the patio crowds and settles into something quieter: fireplaces instead of sunset decks, longer chats at the tasting bar, and the chance to try late-harvest pours and ice wines without a summer waitlist.

Winter wine tasting in Michigan is real — but it is not automatic. Some tasting rooms keep steady indoor hours; others shorten days, close midweek, or pause for a few weeks after the holidays. This guide covers what to expect, which regions and wine trails still make a great day trip, cozy indoor options, holiday-season visits, driving tips, and how to confirm hours before you leave the driveway.

What to Expect at Tasting Rooms in the Off-Season

Think of winter as the opposite of peak harvest weekends. You will usually find:

  • Fewer crowds. Walk-ins are more realistic, though holiday weekends and wine-club events can still fill a room.
  • Indoor-first experiences. Patios may be closed or lightly used; the tasting bar, lounge, and cellar spaces do the work.
  • Flexible staffing. A room that feels busy in July may run with a smaller team in January — arrivals can feel more personal.
  • Seasonal menus. Expect library bottles, dessert wines, and ice wines where a winery makes them — always ask what is pouring that day.
  • Changing hours. Published schedules on our listings are a starting point. Winter is when those hours shift most often, so verify before you go.

"Winter is when Michigan wine country feels most like a conversation — fewer people in the room, more time with the pour, and a tasting bar that is happy to see you."

— MV Editorial Team

Which Regions Stay Worth Visiting

You do not need to cross the whole state. Pick one region (or two adjacent trails), keep the drive short, and leave buffer for weather. Use the Michigan wineries map or directory to cluster stops, then confirm each room is open that day.

Old Mission & Leelanau (Traverse City)

The two peninsulas north of Traverse City remain the classic winter base. Roads are compact, lodging and restaurants stay open year-round, and many estate tasting rooms keep an indoor presence through the colder months. Start with the Old Mission Peninsula Wine Trail or the Leelanau Peninsula Wine Trail, then compare the two in our Old Mission vs. Leelanau guide if you are choosing a single peninsula day.

Ice wine and late-harvest Riesling fans can browse estates known for those styles in our directory — for example Chateau Grand Traverse (Ice Wine, Late Harvest Riesling) and Black Star Farms — Leelanau (A Capella Ice Wine). Availability varies by vintage and pour list; ask when you arrive, and confirm winter hours on each winery page first.

Fennville & Lake Michigan Shore

Southwest Michigan is an easy winter day trip from Grand Rapids, Holland, or Chicago-area visitors who want shorter lake-effect hops than a full Traverse City weekend. Anchor around the Lake Michigan Shore Wine Trail and the Fennville cluster — estates such as Fenn Valley Vineyards and larger lakeshore stops like St. Julian Winery appear in our directory with tasting-room details. Always call ahead; southwest hours can shift with the holiday calendar too.

Southeast, Petoskey & Beyond

Closer-to-home tasting rooms in Southeast Michigan and northern outposts along Tip of the Mitt can also work for a low-mileage winter afternoon. Check the matching trail pages under wine trails and filter the directory by region. Smaller rooms are more likely to run weekend-only winter schedules — another reason to verify before you drive.

Cozy & Indoor Tasting Options

When the wind is off the bay, lean into spaces built for staying inside:

  • Estate cellars and hilltop tasting rooms on Old Mission — places like Mari Vineyards and Chateau Chantal are designed around substantial indoor rooms, not only patio season.
  • Village and urban tasting rooms such as Left Foot Charley in the Traverse City area, where the visit is already indoors and parking is often easier in town.
  • Sparkling-focused stops like L. Mawby on Leelanau — a compact tasting room that suits a short, warm stop when you do not want a sprawling outdoor campus.

Build two or three stops max on a winter day. Extra buffer beats a rushed third tasting when roads slow down.

Holiday-Season Visits

Late November through New Year’s is the busiest slice of the off-season. Wine clubs host member events, some rooms decorate for the holidays, and local calendars fill with fireside weekends. For dates and seasonal programming, start with our events page — then treat each winery’s own listing as the source of truth for whether the tasting room is open to the public that day.

Gift shopping is a natural winter errand: club sign-ups, sparkling for New Year’s, and ice wine for dessert tables. If you are tasting as a couple or small group, midday weekday visits in December are often calmer than the Saturday before Christmas.

Winter Driving & Planning Tips

  1. Watch lake-effect forecasts. Old Mission, Leelanau, and Tip of the Mitt can change quickly. If a squall is due, shrink the day to town-adjacent stops or reschedule.
  2. Daylight is short. Plan tastings earlier. Finishing before dusk keeps peninsula and rural roads easier.
  3. Designate a driver — or book a tour. Winter roads plus tasting pours are a poor mix. Guided options via traversecitywinetour.com or ideas on Wine Tours take the wheel out of the equation.
  4. Pack layers. Parking lots and short walks between the car and the door are colder than the tasting room looks on Instagram.
  5. Use our trip planner. Lodging-adjacent logistics and seasonal notes live on Plan Your Visit.

How to Check Hours Before You Go

This is the most important winter habit:

  1. Open the winery’s page in our directory (or find it on the map) and read the listed hours and tasting notes.
  2. Treat those hours as a guide, not a guarantee — especially November through March.
  3. Confirm on the winery’s own site or by phone the day before (or the morning of) your visit.
  4. If you are chaining several stops, confirm all of them. One midweek closure can undo a carefully timed loop.

We do not publish separate “winter-only” hour tables here because schedules change. Calling ahead is the reliable move.

Plan Your Winter Wine Day

Ready to sketch a route?

  • Browse the winery directory and filter by region
  • Sketch pins on the Michigan wineries map
  • Compare trails on Wine Trails — especially Old Mission, Leelanau, and Lake Michigan Shore
  • Check events for holiday and winter weekends
  • Use Plan Your Visit and Wine Tours for logistics — or book a guided Traverse City day with Traverse City Wine Tours

Michigan wineries in winter reward a little planning and a flexible mindset. Confirm the hours, keep the route tight, and enjoy the quietest — and coziest — season on the wine trail.
